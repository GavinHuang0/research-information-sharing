# Experiment 5 proposed procedure

**Status as of November 23, 2026. Awaiting authorization before execution.** The study compares tests for the project's out-of-sample forecast gains. Preserve the earlier experiment archives and use a separate Experiment 5 run directory. Keep the statistic, scenarios, test directions, seeds, and decisions fixed before generating simulations.

## Estimand and tests

For each comparison, use $G=6^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^6[1-E(A_{it})/E(B_{it})]$, where $A$ and $B$ are the candidate and reference squared forecast losses. The one-sided null is $G\leq0$. Recompute target-specific MSE denominators in every block draw, and use one draw or multiplier vector for the full date vector of targets and procedures. Estimate marginal 95% interval coverage separately from the one-sided testing decisions.

1. **Benchmark.** Reproduce the centered paired stationary bootstrap with expected block length 13, basic intervals, and Holm adjustment in the family. Compare block lengths 6 and 26 as prespecified sensitivities. Use the existing [bootstrap implementation](../../src/factorcast/bootstrap.py) and the [Experiment 2 specification](../02-bootstrap-history/protocol.md).
2. **Studentized stationary bootstrap.** Use the benchmark's block-13 date draws and divide the centered gain by its estimated standard error in each draw. Use a Bartlett HAC estimate on the first-order contribution of each date to the targetwise MSE-ratio statistic, with lag truncation $q=\lfloor4(n/100)^{2/9}\rfloor$. Use the bootstrap-t distribution for the p-value and interval and Holm for the family. Compute the long-run standard error again from the ordered resampled dates in each draw.
3. **Dependent wild bootstrap and HAC reference.** Apply centered dependent multipliers to the same first-order date contributions. Use a unit-variance Gaussian moving average of length 13 and transform each draw $W_t$ to $(W_t^2-1)/\sqrt{2}$. The same weights apply across all comparisons in a replicate. Studentize using the corresponding kernel long-run standard error. Compare its marginal tests and intervals with a normal approximation using the same standard error. [Shao, §§3–4](https://publish.illinois.edu/xshao/files/2012/11/JASA-DWB.pdf) · [Newey and West](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0055)
4. **Joint inference.** Retain Holm as the reference. Compare Romano–Wolf stepdown based on the maxima of jointly resampled, centered studentized statistics for the remaining tests. Assess any gain in power only after measuring familywise error under complete and mixed nulls. [Romano and Wolf](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00615.x)

Use one-sided tail counts with the finite-draw correction $(1+c)/(1+B)$ for bootstrap p-values. Report zero or invalid denominator and standard-error cases explicitly and do not discard them silently. When a required inference statistic cannot be computed, count the trial as a non-rejection and the interval as a coverage failure in the primary operating characteristics. Also report the failure rates so conservatism from failures is visible.

## Stage A. Exact calibration with simulated losses

Use a fixed family of four tests sharing one candidate and four references, as in Experiment 2 Part A. Simulate joint positive weekly losses for the six targets. Construct each stationary unit-variance Gaussian series $U$ from independent AR(1) processes with the same persistence. Allocate variance shares of 0.25, 0.25 and 0.15 to processes shared by the market, target and procedure respectively, with the remaining 0.35 from a process specific to that target-procedure pair. Use $\exp(0.7U-0.7^2/2)$ to give each unscaled loss mean one. Set target scales to $0.7,0.8,0.9,1.0,1.1,1.2$, retain these for the common candidate and multiply each reference's target scales by $1/(1-g)$ to give it the exact declared gain $g$.

Cross 87 and 156 evaluation dates with AR persistence 0, 0.35 and 0.70, plus these three gain vectors:

| Scenario | True gains in the four comparisons |
| --- | --- |
| Complete null | 0%, 0%, 0%, 0% |
| Mixed null and alternatives | 0%, -3%, 3%, 5% |
| Power | 1%, 3%, 5%, 8% |

Use 2,000 independent simulated paths per cell and 1,999 bootstrap draws per path for the main comparison, with common paths and paired draws across methods. Check Monte Carlo p-value resolution with 9,999 draws on the first 200 paths of the 87-date, moderate-persistence complete-null and mixed-null cells for the benchmark and studentized bootstrap. As a stationary volatility stress, repeat the 87-date moderate-persistence complete and mixed null cells with a common two-state multiplier of 1 or 4, transition probabilities 0.025 from low to high and 0.10 from high to low, and divide by its stationary mean. The state is independent of the log-loss shocks; the true gains stay the same.

Report marginal rejection at nominal 5%, marginal 95% coverage and interval width, familywise false rejection under the complete and mixed nulls, per-test power, and Monte Carlo Wilson intervals for rates. For a method to advance as calibrated for the core scenarios, use prespecified tolerances of an upper 95% Monte Carlo bound at or below 7.5% for the relevant null rejection rate and a lower bound of at least 92.5% for marginal interval coverage. Report stress failures and power tradeoffs with those labels. These tolerances are practical simulation criteria, not mathematical guarantees at 5% and 95%.

## Stage B. Replay the forecasting pipeline

Simulate one coherent 48-industry weekly log-variance path per replicate. Use two independent, unit-variance stationary AR(1) factors with persistence 0.75, six target histories, and two groups of 21 donor series with paired factor loadings. Let target $i$ follow $v_{i,t+1}=0.55v_{it}+\theta_i^\top f_t+\varepsilon_{i,t+1}$, with $\theta_i=0.45(\cos(2\pi i/6),\sin(2\pi i/6))$ and independent Gaussian innovations of standard deviation 0.5. At week $t$ each donor measures $f_t$ with one of the directions $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$, repeated seven times in each group, plus independent Gaussian measurement noise. Start the simulated path with a 100-week warm-up before building the retained histories and outcome shift.

- **Exact equal-risk null.** Set donor noise standard deviations to 1 in both groups. Compare the same two-PC ridge procedure fitted using the six targets plus group A or the six targets plus group B. Swapping the groups leaves the data law and the two forecast procedures exchangeable, so each target's expected loss is equal even though the fits differ. Use the group-A model as candidate and group B as reference.
- **Predictive alternative.** Increase group B's measurement-noise standard deviation to 2, keeping everything else fixed. Estimate the actual population forecast-risk gap on an independent batch of 2,000 simulated paths per horizon, using the known conditional target mean and innovation variance to integrate out the next shock. Report reference risk and Monte Carlo uncertainty. This alternative varies the measurement quality while allowing the fitted forecasts to determine the magnitude of the gain.

Use $L=104$, 52 historical validation pairs, first refit at 156 valid pairs, refits every 13 pairs, and evaluation lengths of 87 and 156. Rebuild the current values, rolling controls and next-week outcomes from each simulated path. Standardize, estimate two PCs, select the ridge penalty from Experiment 4's declared grid, and refit in each chronological window. Use the same estimator with each donor set and unpenalized own-history controls. Use 1,000 independent paths per horizon and signal setting and 1,999 bootstrap draws. Compare the current benchmark and the marginal tests meeting the Stage A criteria. Report each Stage A method's disposition. Stage B addresses calibration for one bounded-history comparison.

Use separate deterministic seed streams for Stage A, Stage B, independent risk estimation, and bootstrap randomization, with root seed `20261123` and reproducible child streams indexed by stage, design cell and replicate. Archive each declared scenario, source and settings, simulation summaries, Monte Carlo intervals, failure counts, and enough per-path results to independently recompute the summaries. Validate exact loss means, chronology, exchangeability, null direction, and shared draws with independent numerical checks once execution is authorized.
